# T2-03 — Simple Construction: Heads, Bodies, Poses

**Tool:** Paper first, then Krita
**Goal:** Learn to build a figure out of simple 3D forms (spheres, cylinders, boxes) rather than drawing outlines from nothing — the Loomis method, simplified — and get it just far enough toward anime proportions to be useful for our project.

You don't need to become a fine-art figure drawer. You need enough construction sense to keep a simple character *on-model* across dozens of animation frames, which is a different (more mechanical, more learnable) skill than expressive life drawing.

## Drills

1. **The Loomis head**: draw a sphere, slice it with a vertical and horizontal center line, add a jaw. Do this 10-15 times from slightly different angles (front, 3/4, profile). This center-line habit is what keeps a face from sliding around as you rotate a character between frames — genuinely one of the highest-leverage habits in this whole curriculum.
2. **Anime-proportion pass**: repeat the Loomis head but push toward simplified anime proportions — larger eye placement (roughly lower-middle of the face rather than true-anatomical-middle), smaller nose/mouth, simplified jaw. Frieren's cast is a good reference target: clean, minimal linework, not overly stylized/exaggerated (compare to more extreme "big shonen" anime proportions — Frieren sits closer to realistic proportions with light stylization).
3. **Simple body construction**: block a figure out of a sphere (ribcage/chest), a smaller sphere or box (pelvis), and simple cylinders (limbs) connected by ball joints. Draw this in 5-6 basic poses: standing, walking mid-stride, sitting, arms raised. Don't add clothes or detail yet — the goal is proportion and weight distribution (where's the balance point?).
4. **One simple original character**: using the above, design one very simple character (or reuse existing OCs if you have one) at a "turnaround"-lite level: front view and 3/4 view, same proportions in both. This is the character you'll animate from Track 3's walk cycle (T3-04) through the capstone (the bouncing prop and flour sack use separate simple props, not this character), so keep the design simple on purpose — simple shapes, minimal detail, easy silhouette to read. Complexity is the enemy of a first animated project.

## A note on "crude" drawing skills

Construction drawing is mechanical and rule-based in a way that rewards practice more directly than freehand sketching does. Most people plateau on "I can't draw" specifically because they never learned construction — they're trying to draw a finished image directly instead of building toward it. This lesson is the fix for that, not a detour from animation.
