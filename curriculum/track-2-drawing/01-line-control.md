# T2-01 — Line & Shape Control

**Tool:** Paper first, then repeat digitally in Krita
**Goal:** Build the raw hand-control that "crude drawing skills" are usually missing — not talent, just mileage. This is drawn straight from [Drawabox](https://drawabox.com/) Lesson 1.

Animation lives or dies on confident, consistent lines and shapes drawn *fast* — a wobbly, hesitant line reads badly at 12 drawings a second. This lesson is deliberately mechanical. It will feel unglamorous. Do it anyway; it pays off in every later lesson.

## Why paper first

Drawing fundamentals transfer better when you're not also fighting tablet-eye coordination. Do the first pass on paper, then repeat the same drills digitally in Krita once they feel natural — that second pass is what teaches you the tablet.

## Drills (do each for real — a few minutes isn't enough; give this an hour or two spread over a few days)

1. **Superimposed lines**: draw a straight line from point A to point B, then draw over it again 5-10 times trying to hit the exact same path each time, building confidence in one continuous motion (not tiny mouse-like nudges). Do this in both directions and at multiple angles.
2. **Ghosted lines**: pick two points, "rehearse" the motion above the page a few times without touching it, then commit to one confident stroke. This is the single most important habit for clean animation linework — commit, don't sketch-scratch.
3. **Ghosted planes**: same idea but connecting dots into simple polygons — builds control over line *length* and *direction* together.
4. **Tables of ellipses**: draw ellipses in a grid, aiming for smooth, symmetric ovals, not lopsided ones. Ellipses show up constantly in animation (eyes, wheels, cups, joints) and are notoriously hard to draw well without practice.
5. **Funnels**: ellipses of progressively changing size along a curving axis — this is secretly a 3D-thinking exercise (think of a horn or a tapering limb) and shows up in Ghibli-style organic shapes (tails, horns, hair strands) constantly.

## Digital pass

Repeat drills 1-4 in Krita with a basic round brush, at a size you can draw somewhat large (don't zoom in and draw tiny — big confident strokes from the shoulder/wrist, not the fingers). If lines feel shaky compared to paper, that's normal; it's purely mileage.

## What "done" looks like

Not perfection — just: your lines are landing close to where you aim them, and your ellipses are recognizably even. If everything still looks scratchy and hesitant after a few sessions, that's fine too — keep this drill in rotation in the background while you move on; it'll keep improving.

## Check-in

Photograph or screenshot a page of your ellipses/lines and describe how the ghosting drill felt — Claude can help spot specific habits (e.g. drawing from the wrist vs. the fingers, consistently egg-shaped rather than round ellipses) that are hard to self-diagnose.
