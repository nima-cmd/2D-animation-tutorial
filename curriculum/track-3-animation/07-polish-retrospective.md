# T3-07 — Polish & Retrospective

**Tool:** either
**Goal:** Finish strong, and set up whatever comes next.

## Optional polish pass on the capstone

Pick zero, one, or two of these — not all of them, this is optional refinement, not a new requirement:

- **Color pass**: flat colors or simple shading on your character/background if you kept things line-only until now.
- **Sound**: even a single ambient loop (wind, footsteps) under your capstone short goes a long way toward making it feel "finished" rather than "an exercise." Krita can import basic audio for timing reference; for real muxing, any simple video editor (or even `ffmpeg`) works fine over the rendered video.
- **A second loop variation**: now that the assets exist, a small variant (different time of day, character reacting differently) is cheap to make and good practice at reusing your own work.

## Retrospective

Go back through [`ledger.html`](../../ledger.html) and fill in a real note for every milestone if you haven't been keeping them updated as you went — specifically:

- Which milestone took the longest, and why?
- What's the one drawing/animation habit that improved the most since Track 2 began?
- Which track pulled its weight the most — and did the pen (Track 4) stop being a fight?
- What felt like busywork vs. what felt like it actually mattered?
- Krita or OpenToonz — where do you want to keep working, and where does each still feel clunky?

This isn't a test — it's the raw material for scoping your *next* project accurately, which is usually the hardest part of a second attempt (first projects tend to be either wildly overscoped or so safe they don't teach anything new).

## Where to go next (ideas, not a plan)

- A slightly longer short (30-60 seconds) with an actual simple story beat instead of a loop.
- Deeper OpenToonz: try its Plastic tool (mesh deformation/tweening) or its built-in compositing (Schematic/FX stages) for effects like glows or light.
- A second character and a simple interaction/dialogue scene (lip sync is a whole new can of worms, in a good way).
- Revisit Track 2's drawing fundamentals with fresh eyes — most people find these much easier the second time around, after the "why does this matter" becomes concrete from having animated something.
- More classic milestones from the tradition: a head turn, a lip-sync test, a run cycle (very different mechanics from a walk), effects animation (water, fire, Frieren-style magic circles).

Check this off. This is the end of the curriculum as written — the natural next step is a new ledger entry (or a new track lesson) scoped to whatever direction the retrospective points toward. Ask your instructor for help scoping it when you get there.
