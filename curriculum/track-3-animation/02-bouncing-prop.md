# T3-02 — First Animation: The Bouncing Prop

**Tool:** Krita
**Goal:** Your first real, complete animated exercise. This is the single most universally-taught first exercise in animation (see Richard Williams, *The Animator's Survival Kit* — [references.md](../../references.md)) — we're keeping its exact structure and reskinning the subject to something Ghibli-flavored instead of a plain ball.

## Pick your prop (pick one, keep it simple)

- A **soot sprite** (susuwatari) from *Spirited Away* / *Totoro* — a simple round black blob with a couple of tiny stick legs. Extremely forgiving to squash and stretch since it's already soft and round.
- A **mana orb** (Frieren-style) — a simple glowing sphere, maybe with a thin trailing wisp. Good if you want to practice a bit of a glow/trail effect too, but the core exercise is identical.
- Plain ball, if you'd rather not think about a design choice yet — totally fine, do the design pass later.

Keep it to one solid-colored, simple round shape. Detail is a distraction in this exercise; the whole point is *motion*, not the drawing itself.

## The exercise

Animate your prop bouncing 2-3 times, losing a bit of height each bounce, on 12fps, using what you learned in T3-01:

1. **Block the arcs first**: on one layer, place dots marking the path of the ball through its full arc(s) — this becomes your spacing guide. Don't animate the actual drawing yet.
2. **Key poses**: draw the ball at the top of each arc (fully round, momentarily "floating" at the peak) and at each impact frame (squashed).
3. **In-betweens**: fill in the frames between keys, following your arc dots for position and gradually easing the spacing — wide gaps in the middle of the arc (fast), tight gaps near the top of the arc (slow) and near impact.
4. **Squash & stretch**: squash flat-ish at each impact frame (1-2 frames, snap back quickly), stretch slightly while falling fast between bounces. Preserve rough "volume" — a squashed ball should look a bit wider to compensate for being shorter.
5. **Play it back on loop.** Does it read as "bouncing" at a glance, or does it look like it's floating/sliding? If it looks off, it's almost always a spacing or timing issue, not a drawing issue — check that impacts are the tightest-spaced, fastest-changing frames.

## Common first-timer mistakes to watch for

- Even spacing throughout (makes it look mechanical/robotic — spacing should vary).
- No squash at all (makes it look like a rigid marble, which is a valid choice for a "rock" but probably not your soot sprite).
- Squashing the *whole* silhouette symmetrically without a clear "down" direction — impact squash should read as being flattened *by the ground*, not just uniformly smaller.

## Check-in

Export this as a short video/gif (`File → Render Animation`) and bring it back. This is a great one to get real feedback on — motion is much easier to critique by watching than describing, and this exercise is short enough to iterate on quickly (expect 2-3 passes before it feels right — that's normal, not a sign you're behind).
