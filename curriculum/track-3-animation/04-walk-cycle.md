# T3-04 — Walk Cycle

**Tool:** Krita
**Goal:** The most famous beginner exercise of all (again from *The Animator's Survival Kit*) — a looping walk cycle, using the character you designed in Track 2 (T2-03, refined into a model sheet in T2-04). Keep that model sheet open on-screen the whole time.

A walk cycle is the classic next step after the ball and the sack because it applies the same spacing/timing/arc/weight principles to something with joints, and because it *loops* — which forces you to make the last frame flow believably back into the first.

## The structure (standard 8-frame walk, side view)

This is the traditional beginner breakdown — you can add more in-betweens later, but 8 key frames is the standard teaching structure:

1. **Contact** (front foot forward, touching ground, back foot still touching ground behind)
2. **Down** (body at its lowest point, weight settling onto the front foot)
3. **Passing position** (legs cross, one knee raised, body at its highest point)
4. **Up** (body rising, about to reach the next contact)
5. **Contact** (opposite foot forward — mirror of frame 1)
6. **Down** (mirror of frame 2)
7. **Passing position** (mirror of frame 3)
8. **Up** (mirror of frame 4) → loops back to frame 1

Use your simple side-view (or 3/4 view, if you're feeling ready) character from Track 2. Keep the design simple — no complex clothing or accessories yet, that's for the capstone.

## Steps

1. Draw the two **contact** poses first (frames 1 and 5) — they define stride length and are mirror images of each other.
2. Draw the two **passing position** poses (frames 3 and 7) — tallest point of the cycle, legs crossing.
3. Fill in **down** and **up** poses — lowest and highest points of the vertical bob.
4. Set timing: a natural walk is roughly 12-16 frames total per full cycle at 12fps (so each of your 8 key poses holds for 1-2 frames, adjust to taste — slower/heavier characters get more frames per pose).
5. Add a slight **arc** to the head's vertical bob (it shouldn't move in a straight up-down line) and make sure arms swing opposite to legs (right arm forward when left leg is forward).
6. **Loop it** — play back on repeat. The seam between frame 8 and frame 1 is the hardest part to get invisible; expect to nudge it a few times.

## What to keep simple (on purpose)

Skip secondary motion (hair, clothing sway) for this pass — get the core body mechanics looping cleanly first. You'll revisit secondary motion once this base cycle feels solid, in the capstone.

## Check-in

Render and share the looping cycle. Common issues worth a second pair of eyes: does the character appear to slide instead of plant weight on contact frames? Does the head bob look mechanical rather than arced? Both are very fixable and much easier to spot from outside.
